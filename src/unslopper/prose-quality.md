# Writing quality, with fidelity first

Review the writing for its reader, purpose and genre. Improve the smallest passage that has a
demonstrable problem. Preserve author voice, dialect, register, intentional rhythm and rhetorical
structure. Writing cues do not establish authorship: abstain, and never give an AI probability.

## Separate problems from cues

- Objective problems need evidence, such as an accidental duplicated word. Confirm intent even then.
- Editorial review cues include empty announcements, emphasis that adds no information, unsupported
  generality, repetitive setup/reveal scaffolding and nouns that obscure the action or accountability.
  State what the passage fails to communicate before proposing an edit. A cue may be appropriate.
- Word choice, passive voice, adverbs, hedges, tricolons, contrasts, sentence length and punctuation
  are not defects by themselves. Do not count them as evidence of AI authorship or ban them.

Ask whether an opening promises substance the next sentence already supplies. Review repeated
"not X, but Y" framing only when it displaces a meaningful distinction. Keep literal contrasts
(permissions versus authentication), intentional parallelism, dialogue and domain terminology.
If a missing actor matters, request clarification; do not invent an actor or replace it with "you".

## Preserve meaning before tightening

- Keep all facts, names, numbers, units, negation, attribution, links, references and citations.
  Do not fabricate detail, examples, evidence, anecdotes or reader behavior to make prose concrete.
- Preserve calibrated uncertainty and scope: "most teams" must not become "teams", "may" must
  not become "will", and an association must not become a causal claim. Shorter is not always better.
- Leave quotations, code, legal text and reference entries intact. Report a concern outside the
  protected span rather than silently correcting quoted language or changing its attribution.
- Keep deliberate contrasts, lists, repeated phrasing and punctuation when they serve the argument.
  Preserve dialect and second-language expression; do not normalize them to a generic house voice.
- Ground each proposed change in the supplied text. If support or intent is unclear, retain the
  passage and explain what a human should check. An unchanged result is valid.

Example: "It is important to note that most teams may need a second review [1]." can become
"Most teams may need a second review [1]." It must not become "Teams need a second review."

During drafting, start with the substance and avoid adding empty signposts. During review, quote
the original passage and explain the reader benefit of each small edit. Compare the result for
factual fidelity and the author's voice, not for a lower detector score or cue count.

These are editorial heuristics, not validated classifiers. Research finds model, genre and editing
effects; aggregate vocabulary shifts do not identify an individual author's process. See the
repository's docs/prose-evidence.md for sources, limits and evaluation plans. Adapted from Michael
